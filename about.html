The
Story

5+ Years Shooting
The Beginning

It started with a borrowed camera and no plan.

Like most self-taught creatives, there was no formal education, no structured curriculum — just a camera, an internet connection, and an unstoppable urge to figure things out. The first shots were rough. The framing was off, the exposure was wrong. But something clicked that wasn't just the shutter. The act of seeing — really seeing — the world through a lens felt different from anything else.

Finding the Craft

From snapshots to intentional storytelling.

Hundreds of hours of self-study followed. Forums, YouTube deep-dives, trial and error at golden hour and in the dead of night. The hobby slowly became a discipline. Every shoot taught something new — about light, about patience, about the decisive moment. The camera became less of a tool and more of a language.

Entering Video

When a single frame wasn't enough.

Still photography opened a door, but moving images blew the walls off entirely. Videography introduced a new dimension — time. Learning to compose for motion, to think in sequences, to marry visuals with sound. The editing suite became a second home; cutting footage, building rhythm, crafting the narrative arc that transforms raw clips into something cinematic.

Going Professional

The hobby became the work. The work became the calling.

What began as a passion project gradually attracted attention. Commissions followed. Clients started seeking out this specific voice, this particular aesthetic. Going professional wasn't a conscious decision as much as an inevitable one — when the thing you love most also happens to be the thing you're good at, it would be a waste not to pursue it fully.

The backbone of any high-volume photo workflow. Lightroom handles culling, cataloguing, and batch colour grading at speed — keeping large client deliveries organised and consistent without sacrificing quality.
